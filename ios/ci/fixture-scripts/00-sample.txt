# The Ferry and the Market

ANA: Welcome back to the show. I'm Ana, and with me, as always, is Ben.
BEN: Hello! Today it's ferries, markets, and why the bread sells out by 6:15 a.m. every single day.
ANA: Quick primer for anyone new: the island has 1,250 people, one ferry, and no bakery. So the bread comes over on the first boat.
BEN: Which costs $4.50 a loaf, up 12.5% since 2024. Is that fair? I honestly don't know.
ANA: Dr. Lindqvist, who studied the route for the U.K. transport board, says it is. "The ferry is the bakery," she wrote. "Without one, you don't have the other."
BEN: [laughs] That's a good line. But let's look at the numbers... because they tell a different story.
ANA: Go on.
BEN: Three things. First, the boat runs at 40% capacity in winter. Second, the stall pays no rent. Third — and this is the one people miss — the baker is the captain's brother.
ANA: No!
BEN: Yes. So when we say "the market decides," what we mean is that two brothers decide over breakfast.
